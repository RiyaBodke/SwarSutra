# SwarSutra

1. The problem
Riyaz is the daily backbone of Hindustani singing, but most of it happens alone. A student practises for hours with no one to say that a komal Ga drifted flat, that Ni crept into Yaman, or that a taan fell apart halfway. Mistakes get repeated and become habits until the next lesson, which may be days away. Taans are also fast and easy to forget, and writing them down in sargam by hand takes time.
Existing apps mostly tell you the nearest note. They don't know the raga, can't judge raga grammar, and don't write down what you sang.
2. Who has it, where and how often
Students of Hindustani and light classical singing, from school children to adult learners, practising at home every day. The problem hits every session, because feedback is absent exactly when the mistakes happen.

3. The voice-first product
Set Sa: the singer sings or hums their Sa, and Tonica fixes the tonic and starts a tanpura drone.
Say what you're practising: "Raga Yaman, alankar, medium speed."
Sing. Tonica tracks pitch live and maps each note relative to Sa.
Raga-aware feedback: it flags raga errors ("Ni komal is not in Yaman") and intonation errors ("Ga is 20 cents flat").
Spoken correction in Hindi, Marathi, or English: "Your Ga was flat. Try again?" The singer answers "repeat," "slower," or "next."
Taan to notation: sing a taan and get the sargam written out with timing, saved to a practice notebook.
Progress: accuracy and stability trends per swara, and a recording of each session.
4. Why voice comes first
The input is the singer's voice and the product's feedback is voice. A singer practising can't use their hands to tap a screen, and looking at a phone breaks the practice. Voice commands ("repeat," "slower," "next raga") keep her in the flow. Singing is the only way to get the data, so it can't be a feature added on top.

What makes it different
Several apps already detect the nearest swara, including Dhwani, SwarSaathi, and Swara AI. Tonica adds raga grammar, spoken coaching, and written notation of what you sang.
Honest limits
Fast taans, glides (meend), and microtones are hard to segment into discrete notes. The demo should start with slow to medium phrases, and I haven't tested how well raga-awareness reduces errors.
A real singer or teacher should test it. Judges who know the music will check correctness.
Say plainly that Tonica supports practice and doesn't replace a guru.
